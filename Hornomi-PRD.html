01

Executive Summary

Hornomi is a native mobile app, for iOS and Android, that helps women in Nigeria understand, track, and manage PCOS — polycystic ovary syndrome — with dignity and without judgment. It gives users a plain-language, non-diagnostic self-assessment; simple daily tracking of cycles, symptoms, weight, and mood; a medically reviewed education library; and a light, honest path to professional guidance when they need more than the app can offer.

Hornomi does not diagnose, treat, or replace a healthcare provider. Its job is to close the gap between "I have symptoms and don't understand them" and "I am ready to have an informed conversation with a professional."

02

Problem Statement

PCOS affects a substantial share of Nigerian women of reproductive age — published studies place the figure anywhere from roughly 9% to 20%, higher still among women presenting with infertility — yet many go undiagnosed for years. Symptoms like irregular cycles, acne, weight change, excess hair growth, fatigue, and fertility difficulty are frequently dismissed, misunderstood, or self-managed with no professional input at all.

The barriers are structural as much as medical: specialists concentrated in major cities, high consultation costs, and a real social stigma around reproductive health — including, in some documented cases, the fear of being seen as less "marriageable" because of fertility implications. Most existing tools are either generic global period trackers with no PCOS depth, or general wellness content with no Nigerian context. A woman managing this condition today is left to piece it together from social media, forums, and memory.

03

Solution Overview

Hornomi brings four capabilities into one app:

1 · Understand

A non-diagnostic self-assessment that explains, in plain language, whether a symptom pattern is commonly associated with PCOS.

2 · Track

Structured, low-effort logging of cycles, symptoms, weight, and mood that reveals real patterns over time.

3 · Learn

A medically reviewed education library that replaces fragmented, unreliable information.

4 · Prepare & Connect

Shareable health summaries and a lightweight path to professional guidance, without pretending to be telemedicine on day one.

04

Vision & Principles

To become the most trusted companion for women managing PCOS and hormonal health in Africa.

  • Education before assumptions — evidence-based content, never a substitute for a clinician.
  • Localized and dignified — built for Nigerian realities: cost, access, stigma, culture.
  • Empowerment through data — simple pattern recognition a user can act on.
  • Preventive, not alarming — early awareness without fear-based messaging.
  • Trust and privacy by default — this condition carries stigma; the app must never make that worse.

Functional Requirements

MVP features, in priority order. Each ships to both iOS and Android from a shared design system.

Registration & Authentication

Must Have

Email or phone sign-up with OTP verification; native biometric login (Face ID / Touch ID on iOS, BiometricPrompt on Android) once enabled.

  • One account per verified email/phone; passwords never stored in plain text.
  • 5 failed OTP attempts trigger a 15-minute lockout.
  • Account must be verified before any tracking feature is accessible.

Onboarding, Consent & Adaptive Profile

Must Have

A short, linear flow that only asks follow-up questions relevant to what the user says she wants help with — no consent, no proceeding.

  • Consent is mandatory; every health-disclosure question includes "I prefer not to say."
  • Consent timestamp and policy version recorded before any data collection.
  • Preferences editable later from Profile.

Non-Diagnostic PCOS Assessment

Must Have

A structured questionnaire, built on a recognized clinical screening framework and signed off by a Medical Advisor, that returns a plain-language, categorical result — never a diagnosis.

  • Result always shown with a visible disclaimer and a "Talk to someone" call to action.
  • A defined, Medical Advisor-approved escalation message displays for any flagged answer pattern.
  • Retakes are saved to history, never overwriting a prior result.
Safety requirementThe escalation path for a concerning answer pattern is a launch blocker, not a later refinement. It must be clinically reviewed and testable before release.

Dashboard

Must Have

One glance at cycle, symptom, weight, and mood trends, over a selectable 30/90/180-day range, with a guided empty state for new users.

Cycle, Symptom & Weight Tracking

Must Have

Two-tap-or-fewer logging throughout. Next-cycle prediction shown only once two cycles exist, clearly caveated as an estimate.

Mood Check-in

New

A single-tap, structured mood scale (not just emoji) on the dashboard, with an optional one-line note — a low-effort way to track emotional wellbeing alongside physical symptoms.

  • Never framed as a mental-health diagnostic tool.
  • Repeated low-mood entries within a Medical Advisor-defined window surface a gentle, supportive resource message — once per interval, never punitive.

Health Journal

Should Have

Private free-text entries, excluded from any shared summary unless explicitly opted in per entry.

Educational Content Library

Must Have

Searchable, categorized articles. Every article is medically reviewed before publishing, with review status visible.

Health Summary — Generate & Share

Must Have

Auto-compiles the assessment result and tracked trends into a shareable document for a doctor's visit. Journal entries are opt-in per generation, never included by default.

Request Guidance

New

A lightweight, asynchronous bridge to professional input — not a diagnosis, not real-time telemedicine. A user submits a structured query, auto-populated from her latest assessment and recent logs, to a small pool of verified partner providers, who respond within a stated window (target: 48 hours).

  • Only verified/credentialed providers may receive requests.
  • The user reviews exactly what data is being shared before sending.
  • Clear, unmissable notice: this is not for emergencies.

Notifications & Reminders

Should Have

Native push via APNs (iOS) and FCM (Android), requested through the platform's standard permission prompt. Every non-critical category is individually toggle-able.

Settings, Privacy & Discreet Mode

Must Have

Profile, notification, and privacy controls, plus two features built specifically for the stigma this condition carries.

Discreet Mode & App Lock An alternate app name and icon (using iOS's Alternate App Icons and Android's icon-alias support) so the app doesn't announce its purpose on a home screen, plus PIN/biometric App Lock. Off by default, offered clearly during onboarding's privacy step — not buried in a settings menu.

Business Rules

  • Users may only view or edit their own records; no future dates on any log.
  • The assessment result is never labeled a diagnosis; a flagged pattern always triggers the reviewed escalation message.
  • Journal entries and unshared data stay private unless explicitly opted into a summary or guidance request.
  • All educational content is reviewed and approved by a qualified Medical Advisor before publishing.
  • Consent is recorded, with version and timestamp, before any health data is collected.
  • Only verified, credentialed providers may receive Guidance requests.

Privacy & Compliance

Health data is classified as sensitive personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023. This requires:

  • Explicit, recorded consent as the legal basis for all health-data processing.
  • A Data Protection Impact Assessment completed before public launch.
  • A documented decision on data hosting location and any cross-border transfer.
  • Planning for registration with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission — likely required given health-sector processing and realistic early user volume.
  • A Medical Advisor-approved escalation protocol for concerning assessment or mood input.
  • Data export and permanent deletion available to users at any time.
